01 / phase 1 / three directions

Wild & Wired OS: three visual directions

KDE Plasma 6.6, Wayland, Kubuntu 26.04 LTS base. Each direction below is the same palette, the same five type faces and the same voice, differing in personality and in structure. Every sheet shows the login screen, the desktop on the sponsor wall wallpaper, and the panel with the menu open at 1:1. Pick one and I build Phase 2 on it.

type

voice
a competent friend at a workbench, second person reassurance is structural: "nothing is written to disk" sentence case prose, mono labels uppercased by the component no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no emoji never the words weird, hacker, cyber, guru, just, simply
!! hard in plasma, and what I did about it
Body weight 340. Source Sans 3 ships variable, but Plasma resolves UI fonts to a static family and Qt rounds to the nearest available weight. Ship the static Light (300) and Regular (400) instances and set UI text to 400 at 10pt; keep 340 for the Field Guide and Welcome apps, which are QML and can load the variable file. No red for negative states. KDE's colour scheme has mandatory NegativeText and NegativeBackground roles, and Discover, System Settings and Calamares all use them. I map negative to max-emphasis white on a #03252a fill with an outline plus a `!!` sigil, and every negative state also states its meaning in copy. Flagged: any distro app that hardcodes red is out of our control. The 1.27:1 surface lift. Plasma leans on surface contrast for panel, popup and card separation, and the brand deliberately does not. Hierarchy comes from the #0f5f63 hairline and from spacing, which means the Plasma SVG frames need real 1px strokes, not a soft shadow, and shadow use stays at shadow-2 or lower. #12837b carries no text. Fine for us, but it must be the border and separator role only in the colour scheme. Any role Plasma will render text on gets an ink value, never structure teal. The logo needs a light plate. The supplied lockups contain navy and white artwork that dies on the base. Every appearance sits on a #f4faf9 plate, so the GRUB, Plymouth, SDDM and KSplash marks are plated too. A single-colour mint glyph version of the badge is needed for the 16 and 22px icon sizes, since the raster badge is unreadable below 32px. That glyph is new artwork and I will draw it in Phase 2. The two brand faces are now real files. Rebel Script and Circuit arrived as fonts, so Wild and WIRED are live type on the sheets below instead of cropped raster. That changes two things for the build. First, the boot chain can set them: GRUB renders PF2 fonts converted with grub-mkfont, Plymouth draws text through Pango, and SDDM and KSplash are QML, so all four can carry the real faces at display size. Second, they are the only two faces whose redistribution I cannot confirm. Send the license or the purchase record before the ISO ships. Fallback if it does not clear: the plated raster lockup, which is already how the mark appears here, and Archivo 700 for the version line. Neither brand face is a UI font. Qt will happily set a menu in Circuit and it will be unreadable. Lock this at build time: the colour scheme and kdeglobals name Archivo, Source Sans 3 and JetBrains Mono only, and the brand faces appear solely in wallpapers, boot art, the login and lock headers, and the Welcome and Field Guide hero blocks. Circuit is uppercase with a 15px floor and needs a glyph coverage check for digits and punctuation before it sets any version string. Texture is gradient-implemented. The 24px grid and 4px scanline are the only two permitted background treatments, and both are gradients under the hood. In Plasma they become tiling PNG or SVG patterns in the Plasma style, not live gradients. Keep them out of window backgrounds, where they fight text. No looping animation, except the block cursor. Plymouth is script driven and Plasma's cursor blink is a system setting, so this is fine. It does mean the boot splash is a staged reveal, not a spinner loop. Body measure 66ch. Meaningless in a file manager or settings window the user resizes. It survives only where we own the layout: Welcome, Field Guide, Calamares slideshow.
1A

Ridge & Hollow

appalachian, hand built

The machine as a made object. Ridgelines are drawn as flat silhouettes cut from the base ramp, never shaded, so the wallpaper is a horizon rather than a picture, and the sponsor wall sits on it like a board nailed to a porch post. Chrome is the most generous of the three: a 48px panel, 6px radii, 40px popup padding, and menu rows at 48px with 17px labels, because the person most likely to be frightened by this desktop is the person it is for. The hand built feeling comes from structure, not ornament: hairline contour rules, mono tier rails, and a deliberate asymmetry where the sponsor board hangs from the top of the field rather than centring in it. Warmest and slowest of the three, and the one that photographs best on a keepsake disc sleeve.

1B

Trunk Line

wired, infrastructural

The desktop as a wiring diagram you are allowed to read. Everything sits on the 24px monospace grid, and the sponsor zones are boards on a trunk line, joined by 1px traces with square nodes, so the tier hierarchy is legible as topology before you read a single label. Chrome is tightened to 40px panel, square and 2px corners, and mono metadata is promoted: the login screen states the hostname, kernel and session, the way a machine that respects you states its own configuration. Densest and most confident of the three. It flatters the sysadmins in the room and stays legible for the switchers because the grid gives every element an obvious place, but it is the direction where a careless Phase 2 could drift toward the Matrix cliché, so the trace work stays in structure teal and never mint.

1C

Quartermaster

sovereign, utilitarian

Issued equipment, not a product. The wallpaper is a specification plate: a hairline frame, a mark, and a typographic block that says what this machine is, who paid for it and what it will not do to your disk, with the sponsor tiers set as a plain table because a table is honest about rank in a way a collage is not. Every control is labelled and every list row carries its keyboard shortcut, so the desktop teaches its own operation. No texture anywhere except inside the plate. This is the most legible of the three at 1366x768 and by far the calmest under stress, which matters most on the partitioning screen, and it is the direction that ages best because there is nothing in it to go out of fashion. The trade is charm: it is a deliberately plain object, and the Appalachian half of the brand survives only in the mark and in the voice.

The menu button carries the word MENU, because a switcher looking for Start looks for a word. Rows are 48 to 56px, every power action is labelled, and shortcuts show on the right. Panel 44px, menu popup 560 x 580, radius 4px, popup padding 16px, icons 20px in menu and 18px in panel and tray. Clock is time plus date on one line at 1920, stacked at 1366.
build notes, 1C
Plasma style: 4px frames, 1px #12837b strokes, no texture in chrome. Closest of the three to stock Breeze geometry, so the least likely to break in an app we do not theme. Window decoration: 40px title bar, 4px top corners, focused #12837b border, unfocused #0f5f63 with the title at #8fb3b2. Cost: low. The wallpaper is type and hairlines, so all four resolutions are one SVG with a text layer. Risk: labelled buttons are wider, so the panel needs the task manager to fall back to icons only below 1440px wide. Also the plainest, so it needs the mark used generously to stay branded. Brand faces: the full wordmark set in live type in the plate header, which is the only place two brand faces are allowed in one phrase, plus one script word in the thesis line. Because the plate is type and hairlines, this direction is the one that most needs the license cleared.
what is shared across all three
Panel at the bottom, menu button bottom left, clock bottom right, tray where a Windows switcher expects it. No dock in any direction. Sponsor logo boxes sit on a #f4faf9 plate, because logos arrive in unpredictable colours and a near white plate accepts a dark wordmark cleanly. Any logo delivered white only gets the dark plate variant, #03252a, flagged per record in the data file. Placeholder boxes are neutral, labelled with tier and dimensions, and no invented sponsor brands appear anywhere. The front lockup is the supplied OS logo at one size per surface, on the #f4faf9 plate its own artwork requires, with a mono version slot under it. The slot takes the release year, so v2026 becomes v2027 with a string change and no new art. Quiet strip wallpaper, zero sponsor state, lock screen and installer slideshow reuse the same tier boxes at the same dimensions. Phase 2 draws all of them. Five faces ship: Archivo, Source Sans 3 and JetBrains Mono as the UI system under OFL, and Rebel Script and Circuit as display art, pending the license confirmation flagged above.
